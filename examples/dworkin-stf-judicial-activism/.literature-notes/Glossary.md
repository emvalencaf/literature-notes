# Research Glossary

## Language

#### Judicial Activism

A court departing from the strict, literal application of an existing rule
by weighing competing principles instead — legitimate, in this research's
adopted sense, only when the weighing is reconstructible as an argument from
the constitutional text and prior doctrine, per [[dworkin-1986]]'s
rule/principle distinction.

_Avoid_: Judicialization of politics (a related but distinct phenomenon —
courts being asked to decide political questions at all — this research
takes no position on that separately); [[Glossary#Decisionismo]] (the
failure mode this research treats as activism's opposite, not its synonym).

#### Decisionismo

A ruling that reaches its outcome mainly from the judge's own conviction,
without an argument that could be reconstructed from the constitutional
text, precedent, or a stated principle-weighing — per [[streck-2014]]'s
critique. In this research, a decision counts as *decisionismo*, not
legitimate [[Glossary#Judicial Activism]], whenever its principle-weighing
cannot be restated as a chain of reasons a different judge could evaluate.

_Avoid_: Judicial activism (this research treats the two as opposites, not
degrees of the same thing — see [[decisions/2026-09-15--incorporate-streck-critique]]).
