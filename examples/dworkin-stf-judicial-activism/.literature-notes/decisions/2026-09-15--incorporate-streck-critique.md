## Context

`RESEARCH.md`'s original theoretical framework treated
[[dworkin-1986]]'s rule/principle distinction as a sufficient test for
legitimate [[Glossary#Judicial Activism]]: if a court is weighing
principles rather than mechanically applying a rule, that weighing counts
as activism in the legitimate sense the dissertation adopts.
[[streck-2014--p89--ativismo-judicial]] and
[[streck-2014--p112--decisionismo]] put pressure on that: Streck argues an
opinion can *name* a principle-weighing without the weighing being
reconstructible as an argument, which the original framework had no way to
flag as anything other than legitimate activism.

## Decision

Broaden the theoretical framework: legitimate
[[Glossary#Judicial Activism]] now requires not just that a court invokes
competing principles, but that the weighing is reconstructible as an
argument from the constitutional text and prior doctrine. A ruling that
invokes a principle without that reconstructibility is
[[Glossary#Decisionismo]], not activism, regardless of the vocabulary the
opinion itself uses. `RESEARCH.md`'s Objectives and Initial Theoretical
Framework sections were updated accordingly, and `Glossary.md` gained the
[[Glossary#Decisionismo]] entry.

## Consequences

Every STF opinion reviewed from this point forward needs to be checked
against reconstructibility, not just against "does it cite a principle" —
this is a stricter bar than the original framework implied. It doesn't
change the reading of [[dworkin-1986--p52--regra-vs-principio]], since that
note only describes Dworkin's own model and takes no position on STF
practice; it does mean any future note that credits an STF opinion with
legitimate activism must show its reconstructibility, not just its
vocabulary.
